Many new developers think the best way to communicate with the user is through clear messages. But it's much better when we benefit from the human senses.


Visual is just one channel. There's also haptic (vibration) and auditory (sound) feedback. Smartphones already give us all three, ready to use.

System Feedback shows up in a few common forms:
* toast messages and color coding
* vibration on key actions
* sound cues for notifications
* loading spinners and progress bars

I'm building Fonli, my AI-powered language learning app, and I shipped the first version with just the core functionality. No polish, no extra touches. And that includes System Feedbacks. The goal was to get it in front of real users fast.

Then real feedback started coming in. People testing Fonli said they missed a clear confirmation after finishing an exercise. So I added visual and haptic feedback: Green or red depending on the answer, plus a soft vibration when it's done.

That small addition changed how the app felt. Suddenly it felt alive, not just functional.
