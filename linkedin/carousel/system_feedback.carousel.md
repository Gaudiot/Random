# 🎠 Carrossel — System Feedback

---

# Slide 1 — What is it?

## Title

### **What Is System Feedback, Really?**

## Body

System feedback isn't just a clear message on screen.

It's anything that talks to your senses.

Smartphones today can cover three channels:

* visual
* haptic
* auditory

(taste and smell aren't on the roadmap yet)

---

# Slide 2 — Visual Feedback

## Title

### **Visual Feedback**

## Body

The most common channel — anything the user sees.

* toast messages
* color coding
  → green for success, red for failure

* warning modals
  → confirm before an important action, like canceling

Simple, but effective.

---

# Slide 3 — Haptic Feedback

## Title

### **Haptic Feedback**

## Body

Feedback you feel, not see.

Most phones can vibrate on demand — great for users who keep their phone on silent.

* light vibration
  → confirms a button press

* strong vibration
  → signals something important or that needs attention

It reassures the user the action actually registered.

---

# Slide 4 — Auditory Feedback

## Title

### **Auditory Feedback**

## Body

Sound is the fastest way to grab attention.

Common uses:

* a notification sound → new LinkedIn message
* a distinct tone → correct vs incorrect answer in Fonli

Great for alerting users to something that just happened in a tab that is unfocused.

---

# Slide 5 — Loading States

## Title

### **Don't Leave Users Guessing**

## Body

Waiting without feedback feels broken, even when nothing is wrong.

iFood example: a loading screen with messages while your order is placed. Tells you the app didn't crash, just be patient.

Sometimes it can be simpler:
* a spinner
* a progress bar

Either way, feedback increases confidence.

---

# Slide 6 — My Fonli Story

## Title

### **How I Learned This The Hard Way**

## Body

Fonli is currently in testing with real users.

To ship early, I launched with just the core functionality. No extra polish.

Then feedback started coming in: people wanted confirmation that an exercise was actually completed.

So I added:

* visual → green/red depending on the answer
* haptic → a soft vibration on completion

---

# Slide 7 — Mix, But With Moderation

## Title

### **Combine Channels Carefully**

## Body

Don't rely on a single feedback type.

Example: a wrong answer could combine

* red color + sad emoji (visual)
* medium vibration (haptic)
* an error sound (auditory)

But more isn't always better. Stacking too many at once can cause sensory overload.

---

# Slide 8 — Takeaway

## Title

### **Informative, Not Invasive**

## Body

Well-built feedback makes UX grow exponentially.

It can directly boost engagement and retention — which matters a lot in today's market.

The rule I try to follow:

Good feedback should be informative, not invasive.

That balance is everything.
